Building 2026

Carbon Protocol

A carbon-aware home pilot. You give it deadlines, like the dryer done by 7 a.m. It watches live grid carbon intensity and your electricity rate, picks the cleanest cheap window, runs the device, and reports the dollars and CO2 saved with the method spelled out.

Watch the original pitch

The idea

From a pitch to a pilot

In April 2025 I pitched the Carbon Internet Protocol for the Amazon Devices Climate Tech Accelerator. The point still holds: household energy is over 20% of global emissions, people cannot see their impact in real time, and smart devices are blind to carbon. The demo line was an Alexa that says running the dryer now will emit 35% more CO2 than waiting until 9 p.m., and offers to schedule it.

CarbonPilot is that pitch narrowed to something shippable in weeks: a phone and voice app that shifts flexible home loads to hours when the grid is cleaner and power is cheaper. The wedge is the load thermostat programs do not cover well, plug loads and laundry plus dishwasher orchestration across brands, including renters who cannot install a smart thermostat. No new hardware is needed to start. It connects to devices people already own.

Four parts of the original pitch are out of the MVP on purpose. Blockchain logging is out; the audit trail is a hash-chained signed log in Postgres, which gives auditability without a chain to operate. The full digital twin is out; a lightweight home model covers device wattage, runtime, and deadlines. Token rewards are out; streaks and neighborhood comparison tied to real savings are cheaper to run and easier to explain. On-device AI is deferred; inference runs server side, and no audio or video leaves the home.

Architecture

Grid signal in, scheduled loads out

Detail

At a glance

StackFastAPI, Pydantic, Postgres with Timescale, Alembic migrations, Redis queue, Docker Compose, pytest.
GridElectricity Maps primary, EIA-930 fallback with a same-hour-yesterday persistence forecast, synthetic curve for demo and tests. Sources are tried in order and a failing source falls through to the next. Synthetic readings are labelled synthetic everywhere, including on the report.
SchedulerInterval packing over a 24 hour horizon in 15 minute slots. Minimizes a weighted mix of carbon and cost, and enforces breaker headroom so concurrent loads stay under the NEC 80% continuous load rule.
ReportMonthly kWh shifted, kg CO2 avoided, and dollars saved, with baseline, method, and data sources printed. CSV and PDF export. Stated limits: savings are planned, not metered; the baseline is run now rather than a recorded week of behavior; average emissions only, no marginal figure yet.
Audit logHash-chained, signed append-only log per household, in Postgres. Auditability without a chain to operate.
AuthPer-household API tokens, stored as hashes only, so a lost token cannot be recovered. Optional admin token for household creation.
APIHouseholds, devices, runs, grid readings, reports, and export and delete of a household's data. One page dashboard at the root, and an interactive what-if at /how-it-works that runs the real solver and stores nothing.
MCPOne tool, reschedule_appliance: move a named appliance to the cleanest window before its deadline.
StageWeek 1 built Nov 14, 2026: grid signal, deadline scheduler, verified report, plugin interfaces, auth, run executor, migrations, and the how-it-works page. Runs locally and in Docker Compose. No live deployment yet. Real device adapters (Kasa/Tapo, Home Assistant, EV), WattTime marginal emissions, Alexa skill, and push notifications not started. Source repo is private.
